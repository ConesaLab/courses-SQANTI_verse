## 🧪 **Section 3: Splice Site Accuracy & Canonical Junctions**

6. **What percentage of novel isoforms in catalog (NIC) and novel not in catalog (NNC) have all canonical splice junctions (`all_canonical == "canonical"`) in each run?**  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   - **IsoQuant (*de novo*)**: 38 out of 43 NNC isoforms (88.4%) have all canonical splice sites.
   - **FLAIR**: All 25 NNC isoforms (100%) have canonical splice sites, as FLAIR's correction module corrects or removes non-canonical splice junctions unsupported by short-read data.
   </details><br>

7. **Why does FLAIR detect 0 Fusion transcripts, whereas IsoQuant detects 2 Fusion transcripts?**  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   By default, `flair align` filters out supplementary alignments (`--filtertype removesup`). Fusion transcripts require keeping or separately analyzing supplementary alignments (chimeric reads). IsoQuant retains supplementary read alignments during genome mapping, allowing SQANTI3 to detect multi-gene fusion transcripts (such as `transcript1012` joining `ENSMUSG0000052595` and `ENSMUSG00000118366`).
   </details><br>

## 🧠 **Section 4: Critical Thinking & Method Selection**

8. **Scenario A:** You are working on a non-model organism without a high-quality reference genome annotation GTF. Which transcript reconstruction strategy should you use, and what are its trade-offs?  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   
   **Strategy:** *De novo* transcript reconstruction (e.g., IsoQuant without reference GTF).  
   **Trade-offs:** 
   - **Pros:** Unbiased discovery; does not depend on prior annotation; can discover completely novel gene loci and isoform models.
   - **Cons:** Higher rate of unassigned gene identifiers (`novel_gene_...`); higher sensitivity to read degradation artifacts at 5'/3' ends; may overestimate novel unannotated isoforms.
   </details><br>

9. **Scenario B:** You are studying human disease samples with deep short-read RNA-seq and long-read PacBio/ONT data, and you want to minimize false-positive novel isoform discovery. Which tool and strategy would you recommend?  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   
   **Strategy:** FLAIR with reference GTF and short-read junction guidance (`flair align` + `flair transcriptome --junction_tab`).  
   **Reasoning:** FLAIR integrates orthogonal short-read splice junction data to correct misaligned long-read splice sites and validates novel junctions against short-read coverage, offering high precision and eliminating false-positive novel isoforms caused by long-read sequencing noise.
   </details><br>

10. **Summary Question:** In your own words, summarize the main trade-off between **Sensitivity** (detecting maximum possible isoforms) and **Precision** (detecting only true, highly supported isoforms) across the three methods.  
    <details><summary>Answer</summary>

    - **IsoQuant with Reference:** Highest **Sensitivity** (551 isoforms, 446 FSMs). Best for comprehensive isoform cataloging when reference annotations exist.
    - **IsoQuant *de novo*:** Balanced **Unbiased Discovery** (468 isoforms). Best when reference annotations are missing or incomplete.
    - **FLAIR with Short Reads:** Highest **Precision** (432 isoforms, 60 novel). Best for conservative, high-confidence isoform discovery validated by short-read splice junction evidence.
    </details>
